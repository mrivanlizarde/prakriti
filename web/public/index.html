How it works

From a raw recording to a published piece.

Recordings save where they save today, and Prakriti finds them there. After a one-time studio setup, the only routine step is a 10-second test recording before each session, played back to check there is sound. Anything else, such as a phone recording, goes into the same shared folder.

  1. Step 1

    Raw material arrives.

    A studio recording saves into the shared Studio Recordings folder, as it does today. Archive recordings arrive from the archive drive.

  2. Step 2

    Prakriti waits until the file is complete, then checks it.

    It waits for the file to finish syncing and stop growing, then confirms it opens and has sound. If a recording is silent, you hear about it the day Prakriti’s Mac sees it. Clips under a minute are filed as tests.

  3. Step 3

    It transcribes and labels who is speaking.

    Gabriel and Serenity are the only voices Prakriti can know, and only once each of them agrees. Any other voice is treated as a student’s and cut, and Ivan listens before anything goes out.

  4. Step 4

    It cuts, masters and packages.

    Setup chatter is trimmed and the sound is set to the level podcast apps expect. Titles, show notes, chapters, verdicts, the accuracy checklist and the privacy receipt are drafted.

    episode.mp3 · 55:39 · level set for podcast apps · transcript · show notes

  5. Step 5

    It asks for your yes.

    One review screen, one step to approve. Everything on it can be changed or cut first. For now notices come by email: one when your pieces are ready for your yes, and one if a piece needs a decision. Later, one notification per recording on your phone and Mac, updated as it moves.

Privacy and accuracy

Built around two conditions.

Serenity raised them on 24 Sep: students’ privacy, and accuracy. Until she sets the exact rule, Prakriti keeps every student out of anything public.

  • An unknown voice is treated as a student.

    Every recording so far has one shared microphone, and on one microphone software can mistake a student for a teacher without knowing it. Nothing from a session where students were present goes public, whether they spoke or only listened, until Serenity decides otherwise; the first pieces are talks where one teacher speaks alone. Any voice not clearly Gabriel’s or Serenity’s is cut, and Ivan listens at the moments Prakriti is least sure about before anything goes out.

  • Names it finds are muted.

    Prakriti looks for students’ names, mutes each one it finds and holds anything it is unsure of. Every muted name is marked on the privacy receipt by its time, never written out. A name it misses cannot be marked, so Ivan listens at the queued moments, including the opening and closing minutes, where names are said, before anything goes out.

  • Classes wait for Serenity’s rule.

    For now, a class goes public only with every student removed, and only after Serenity sets her rule, a lawyer has read a new class release the students have agreed to, and she approves that piece’s privacy receipt. Whether a student who says yes in writing may ever be heard is Serenity’s rule to set.

  • Who approves what.

    Each teacher approves their own pieces, or names someone who may. Serenity approves every piece in her voice for now, and the privacy receipt of anything in which a voice other than the teachers’ was found. Pieces that name Serenity, or refer to her, are shown to her first; if she has not looked within her hold time, the passage about her is cut. Ivan listens at the few moments of each piece most worth a listen before it reaches the approver. Nothing is approved early to meet a date.

  • Every piece has an accuracy checklist.

    Five to ten checkable claims, such as dates, names, numbers and Sanskrit terms, each with its exact quote and time. Publishing waits until each one is resolved. Archive pieces also carry “Recorded in” with the month and year, and are checked for offers and details that no longer apply.

  • Consent is per person and per use.

    Podcast, YouTube, the Library, Spanish and voice recordings are each a separate yes, and each can be withdrawn. Prakriti never makes an AI voice or face of anyone.

  • Nothing is published without a yes.

    The yes comes from a named person and is tied to exactly what will be published: the file and every word with it. Any change sends it back for review.

  • Any of you can stop it.

    One step pauses all publishing at once, and one step takes a single piece down. Work continues privately while publishing is paused. Until Friday 6 November, Ivan takes one down by hand, at once.

Questions

Questions.

What do Gabriel and Serenity have to do?

Gabriel records as usual, with a 10-second test recording before each session, and says yes to each piece on one screen. Proposed: under 2 minutes of his time per piece, after Ivan has listened. Ivan uploads each approved piece to the podcast and checks it is live; later, Prakriti does. Instagram comes later; nothing is asked of Gabriel there until it does. Serenity meets Ivan in the week of Monday 12 October and again on Tuesday 27 October, and answers the privacy questions; until she does, the most private setting applies. She approves every piece in her voice and the privacy receipt of anything with other voices, and sees first any piece that names her.

Where do the recordings live?

Where they are saved today, in the shared Studio Recordings folder. Prakriti will read from that folder and never write into it. Its own work stays on the Mac that runs it, and only finished files go online.

What if it gets something wrong?

Before anyone approves, the review screen will show the transcript, the verdicts, the accuracy checklist and the privacy receipt. You can say not yet, and why. Ivan makes the change, and nothing goes out in the meantime. Later the screen will let you ask for changes, cut a passage or try again. Transcripts can mishear Sanskrit and names, which is why the checklist exists.

What happens when the Mac is asleep?

For now Prakriti will run on Ivan’s MacBook Pro, so it pauses when the lid is closed and catches up when it wakes. Its status will say so plainly. An always-on home for it is an open question on the roadmap, and the 72-hour target starts only once it has one.

How do I stop it?

Pause All Publishing will sit at the top of Settings for each of you. One step stops every outward action, while work on recordings continues privately. Taking a single piece down will also be one step. Until Friday 6 November, tell Ivan, and he takes one down at once.

What will notifications never show?

A student’s name, or a quote from a session, because notifications show on lock screens. A button that publishes: approving happens on the review screen. Anything about a clip under a minute, which is filed as a test. Until notifications reach phones and Macs, after Friday 6 November, notices come by email, under the same rules, and an email never carries a button that approves.

Will Prakriti ever use an AI voice?

Only a teacher’s own, made by that teacher in their own account, for a script that teacher approved, always labeled as AI. Prakriti never makes one, and never makes an AI face of anyone. Nothing of Serenity’s without her written yes.
